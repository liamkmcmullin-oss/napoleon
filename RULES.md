# Napoleon — How to Play

Napoleon is a trick-taking card game for **4 or 5 players**. One player bids to be **Napoleon**, secretly names a partner card (the **Angel**), and then tries to win enough **point cards** in tricks with that partner's help. Everyone else plays as the defenders.

If you know Hearts, Spades or Euchre, you already know most of it.

---

## 1. The Cards

- A standard 52-card deck plus **1 Joker** (53 cards).
- **Point cards:** Ace, King, Queen, Jack and Ten of every suit — **20 point cards** in total. Each is worth 1 point. Every other card, including the Joker, is worth nothing.
- Normal rank order, high to low: A K Q J 10 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2.

| Players | Cards each | Widow (face-down cards) |
|---------|-----------|--------------------------|
| 4       | 12        | 5                        |
| 5       | 10        | 3                        |

The dealer deals each player their hand and sets the widow aside, unseen. The player to the dealer's left bids first, and the deal rotates left each hand.

---

## 2. Bidding

A bid has two parts: a **number of points** (12 to 20) and a **trump suit** (or No Trump).

- The number is how many of the 20 point cards you promise Napoleon's side will win.
- Bids go around the table starting left of the dealer. On your turn, bid higher or pass.
- **A higher number beats a lower number.** At the same number, suits rank:

  **Clubs < Diamonds < Hearts < Spades < No Trump**

  Example: 13♥ beats 13♦ but loses to 13♠, and 14♣ beats 13 of anything.
- Once you pass, you are out of the bidding.
- Bidding ends when everyone else has passed, or when someone bids **20 No Trump** (the highest possible bid).
- **No all-pass hands.** If everyone else passes before anyone has bid, the last player must bid at least 12.

The winning bidder becomes **Napoleon**, and their suit becomes **trump** for the hand.

---

## 3. The Angel (Napoleon's Partner)

After winning the bid, Napoleon:

1. **Names the Angel card** — any card in the deck (e.g. "the Queen of Hearts"). Whoever holds that card is Napoleon's partner. Nobody else learns who that is until the card is played.
2. **Picks up the widow** and adds it to their hand.
3. **Discards** the same number of cards face-down (5 in a 4-player game, 3 in a 5-player game), leaving a normal-sized hand.

Things to know:

- The Angel is **secret**. The Angel knows they hold the named card, but Napoleon doesn't know who that is, and neither does anyone else. The Angel is revealed when they play the named card.
- If Napoleon names a card that they hold themselves, or that was in the widow, Napoleon has **no partner** and plays alone (see "Scoring" — this pays double).
- Point cards Napoleon discards **do not count** for anyone.

---

## 4. Playing a Hand

Napoleon leads the first trick. Each trick, every player plays one card, and the winner leads the next. Play goes clockwise.

### Following suit

You must **follow the suit that was led** if you can. If you have none of that suit, you may play any card.

### Trump

- Trump cards beat non-trump cards.
- **The Jack of trump is the highest trump**, followed by the **other Jack of the same colour** (the "sister Jack"), followed by A, K, Q, 10, 9 … 2.
  - Example: if Hearts are trump, the order is J♥, J♦, A♥, K♥, Q♥, 10♥ …
- The sister Jack **counts as a trump card**, not as a card of its printed suit. You must play it if trump is led and you have nothing else that's trump.
- **Trump does not exist on the first trick.** On trick 1, all suits are plain and nothing is trump.
- In a **No Trump** hand, there is no trump suit at all.

### Winning a trick

Check these in order — the first one that applies decides the winner:

1. **The Ace of Spades always wins** the trick.
2. **A led Joker wins** (see "The Joker" below).
3. **The Two rule:** if a **2** is played — by anyone, not only the leader — and **every** player follows suit (no trumping or Joker), that **2 wins** — the lowest card takes it.
4. Otherwise the **highest trump** wins, if any trump was played.
5. Otherwise the **highest card of the suit that was led** wins.

On trick 1 (no trump), only rules 1 and 5 apply: the Ace of Spades wins, otherwise the highest card of the led suit.

### The Joker

- The Joker can be played **at any time**, even if you could follow suit, *except* that it **cannot be led on the first trick** (unless you have nothing else).
- **If you lead the Joker**, it wins the trick. In a suit hand, the others must follow **trump**. In a No Trump hand, the leader **calls a suit** and the others follow that suit.
- **If you play the Joker on someone else's lead**, it can't win — it just gets rid of a card.
- **Special rule — the Three of Spades:** if someone leads the **3♠** (after trick 1) and you hold the Joker, you **must** play it. This is the Joker's weakness: anyone who leads the 3♠ can force the Joker out, where it wins nothing.

### Winning tricks = collecting points

Whoever wins a trick keeps all its cards. At the end of the hand, each player counts the **point cards** (A, K, Q, J, 10) in the tricks they've won.

---

## 5. Scoring

**Napoleon's side** = Napoleon + the Angel. Add up their point cards. If the total is **at least the bid number**, Napoleon **wins**; otherwise Napoleon **loses**.

**Base score** = (bid − 10) × 10. So a bid of 12 is worth 20, a bid of 15 is worth 50, a bid of 20 is worth 100.

| Outcome | Napoleon | Angel | Each defender |
|---------|----------|-------|----------------|
| Napoleon wins  | **+base**  | +base ÷ 2 | −base ÷ 2 |
| Napoleon loses | **−base**  | −base ÷ 2 | +base ÷ 2 |

**Multipliers** (they stack):

- **Napoleon is their own Angel** (alone): × 2.
- **Napoleon's side takes all 20 points:** × 2.

When Napoleon plays alone, there is no Angel share: Napoleon gets ±(2 × base) and **every** other player gets the opposite of base.

Scores carry over from hand to hand. Play as many hands as you like and compare totals at the end.

---

## 6. Quick Example

*4 players. Maya bids **14 Hearts** and wins the auction.*

1. Maya picks up the 5-card widow, names the **K♠** as her Angel, and discards 5 cards. Sam secretly holds the K♠ — he's the Angel, but Maya doesn't know it.
2. Hearts are trump (except on trick 1). Everyone plays out 12 tricks.
3. Maya's tricks contain 9 point cards. Sam's contain 5. That's **14** for Napoleon's side — exactly enough.
4. Base = (14 − 10) × 10 = **40**. Maya **+40**, Sam **+20**, and the other two players **−20** each.

---

## Cheat Sheet

- **20 point cards:** A K Q J 10 of each suit. Joker is worth nothing.
- **Bid 12–20**, suits rank C < D < H < S < NT.
- **Napoleon** names a secret partner card (the **Angel**), takes the widow, discards.
- **No trump on trick 1.**
- **Trump order:** Jack of trump, sister Jack, A K Q 10 9 … 2.
- **A♠ always wins.** A led Joker wins. A led 2 wins if everyone follows suit.
- **3♠ led** forces the Joker out of a player's hand.
- **Score:** (bid − 10) × 10, Napoleon wins if their side gets ≥ the bid.
